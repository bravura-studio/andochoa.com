It always goes the same way.

During the day I work in parallel. My agents run the task list while I'm deep at the agency — meetings, focus time, head down. They stay quiet when they hit something they need me for, and I can't check back for two hours. So the ideas pile up on one side of my brain while I'm being a PM on the other.

Then I get home. Around 10pm the tiredness kicks in. But I know, in the back of my head, that I just need to go to the computer and write back to my agents. Quick thing. I fool myself every single time — *this'll be fast.* They'll pick up the task, we'll finish it in the morning.

Except something pops up. And then there's another issue to work on. And somewhere in there I get into the zone, and the sleepiness just — goes away.

Just one more round. I swear, it's just one more and then I go to bed.

Then I look at the clock and it's somewhere between 3 and 4am.

Maybe I should go to sleep.

---

Back in July [I wrote](https://andochoa.com/posts/journals/two-years-no-revenue) that I was scared. Not of the job — of what the job would do to the animal spirit. That a paycheck removes the desperation, a routine numbs the freedom, and the fire quietly drops from bonfire to pilot light without you noticing. The fear wasn't that I went back. It was that I'd get comfortable.

So — report back, three months later.

It's 3am and I'm wide awake. The sleepiness went away on its own because an agent came back with something and I had to see it through.

The fire didn't go quiet. It just moved to the night shift.

Which leaves the real question. Why? Why does this keep me up when the job that actually pays me sends me home empty?

---

The honest answer is: it's mine.

At the agency I'm also building — deep in AI, shipping, accountable for whether it works. On paper, the exact same work. But the version of me that comes home with nothing left still finds a second wind at 1am for my own stuff. Same hands. Same tools. Same tired body. The only difference I can name is that this one is mine.

And I know that doesn't fully hold up. As a PM I'm accountable for real products that real people use too. So it's not the stakes, or the accountability, or even people using it. It's just that it's mine. I couldn't put it into words any better than that.

Or maybe "mine" is the wrong word. What I actually mean is that it comes from me.

---

I've always had too many ideas. A dozen threads running at once, wanting to try this and that — and almost all of them dying quietly in my head, because there was never enough time or enough hands to chase them down. For years that was just the background noise of my life: things I'd never get to.

Then I built the system. The agents, the whole machine behind them. And for the first time, the ideas had somewhere to go. They stopped dying in my head and started getting out — turning into something real. Things someone else can use. Or things only I use. Doesn't matter which.

That's the part that's mine. Not the product at the end of it. The fact that something that started as a thought in the shower now exists, because I made it.

That's the craft. The output was never the point. The making was.

---

So is it about the money?

People love to say it isn't. Jason Fried says it — "it ain't the money, as I've been fortunate enough to make more than I'll ever be able to spend." And I believe him. But notice where he's standing when he says it. At the top. After winning. From up there, "it's not about the money" costs nothing to say.

Ask the other guy. Two years in, zero revenue, a day job covering the bills. If a check cleared tomorrow and money was solved forever — I wouldn't stop. I'd double down on whatever's working, harder. Build a moat strong enough to withstand any storm. And then it stops being about building and starts being about preservation. Maybe I build in the real world after that. Bricks and stuff. Keep the cash flow well and kicking — for the kids I have, and the ones that come after them. A legacy they can build on top of, so they get the freedom to choose their own path.

Build. Fun. Free. It was always pointed at that last word. And it was never for me.

That's the difference. Fried can afford to say it's not the money. The broke guy at 3am, building for zero euros, had to earn the right to — the hard way, no safety net, choosing it anyway, night after night, for nothing but the making.

---

So why am I still doing this?

I don't have a cleaner answer than the one my nights already give me. It's 3am again. The laptop's still open.

Maybe I should go to sleep.

Just one more round.

Keep building. -Ochoa
