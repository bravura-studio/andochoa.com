Someone wants my skills. And I'm happy about it.

I'm also sad, because saying yes means leaving a team behind — the people at the agency who took me in when I needed it most. Some of the best people I've found in my whole career. Not long ago I was the anxious one, applying here and there, most of it leading nowhere. They were the ones who said yes. Now I'm the one saying no.

A bigger offer came. Health-tech scale-up, growing, real problems to solve, fifty percent more net. Four months into the agency job and I'm already walking.

Which leaves the only question that actually matters to me right now.

Am I a mercenary?

---

Here's the thing I keep circling.

I switched for the money. I know I built a reason around it — better company, better trajectory, more to learn, no conflict with the thing I build on the side. All true. But I also know how easy it is to lay a clean story on top of a simple motive. So I have to ask it straight: is the reason real, or am I just fooling myself into feeling okay about chasing a bigger check?

I don't fully know. That's the honest answer. And I'd rather sit in the not-knowing than pretend I've got it solved.

---

Then I told them I was leaving. And they asked me to stay.

Of course they did. No one likes to lose good resources.

*Good resources.*

That's the phrase that stuck. Not a friend, not a teammate — a resource. And I sat with it, because it cuts both ways, and the second way is the one that set me loose.

They value me now. Genuinely. I believe that. But phase one just closed, phase two is getting harder to sign, and it might take a month, or two, or never. So play it forward. The crunch comes. There's no phase two. The work runs dry.

Would they keep me anyway? Because they like me? Because they value me?

I've seen this movie. Four layoffs taught me the ending. When the math turns, value doesn't save you. Good resources get released like any other. The affection is real and the balance sheet is realer, and when they disagree, I already know which one wins.

So here's where I landed. They'd keep me if the money's there. I'm leaving because the money's better somewhere else. We're playing the exact same game — I just moved first. That's not me betraying them. That's me reading the board the way they'd read it if our seats were swapped.

---

The mercenary frame only stings because I was holding myself to a loyalty the other side never signed.

And the money was never the point anyway. It's what the money is *for*. There's a thing I'm building with one pilot client and zero revenue that I still can't put down at 3am. The raise doesn't buy a nicer life. It buys that thing more time. More runway. More room to be wrong before I have to be right.

Build. Fun. Free. It was always pointed at that last word. A fifty percent raise is just more Free. This isn't me selling out the mission. It's me funding it.

---

Still. Am I fooling myself?

Maybe. I can't rule it out, and I won't pretend I can.

But here's the test I keep coming back to. A mercenary feels nothing when he leaves. He packs up, takes the bigger coin, doesn't look back. I'm not doing that. Walking away from those people costs me something real. I'm happy and I'm sad in the same breath, and the sad part won't quiet down.

That's the receipt. If I were only in it for the money, goodbye wouldn't hurt. It hurts. So the mission's still in there somewhere — I'm just pointing it where it belongs now. At the people at home. At the thing with one client that keeps me up. At the freedom I'm still trying to buy, one move at a time.

Not a mercenary. Just a guy who finally stopped apologizing for playing the game as well as it gets played on him.

Keep building. -Ochoa
