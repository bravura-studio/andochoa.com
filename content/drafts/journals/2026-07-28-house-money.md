Last weekend I shipped 132 commits. 51 Saturday, 81 Sunday. Then Monday I told the agents to pick up one more.

Two years ago that weekend would have been panic. Ship or die. Every commit a bet against the clock, against the runway, against the voice that says you're too late and it isn't working.

This time it wasn't panic. It was the most alive I've felt in months.

Something changed, and it's not what I would have guessed.

I took a job. PM at an agency. It pays the bills now. I figured that would be the death of the dream — the founder who "just needs some stability" and quietly never ships again.

It did the opposite.

When the bills stopped riding on the build, the build got lighter. I don't need striva-dental to save me. I need it to be good. Those are different jobs, and the second one is the one I actually love.

I've run the other version. Striva-app: nine hundred commits in a year. Real product, real users, fewer than ten of them. Barely a dent in the bills. A year of shipping into a quiet room.

The old me would call that failure.

The new me went and looked. Every builder I admire took multiple swings over multiple years before one of them caught fire. The rockets are the exception. The rule is: you build, it lands quiet, you build again. Nobody writes the thread about the eight that didn't work.

So now I'm playing with house money.

Friday, striva-dental goes in front of its first real client. A dentist who didn't sign up to be my experiment. Maybe it's the one. Probably it isn't. Either way I get to keep doing the thing.

I still daydream about the explosion. The overnight that quietly took five years. I won't pretend I don't.

But I don't need it tomorrow anymore.

I like to build. Anything. That was always the engine — I just couldn't hear it over the runway running out.

Now the runway isn't my problem.

Let it compound.

Keep building. -Ochoa
