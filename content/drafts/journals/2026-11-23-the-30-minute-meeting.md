She asked for the meeting. Said she wanted to align, talk about the future.

I told her to pick the day. Any day, any time. I'd be there.

She moved it three times. The third one she just didn't show. No message. I had to chase her to find out she wasn't coming.

We started this two years ago. Her and me. Close on every project, trading problems back and forth, building Striva out of nothing.

She works hard. I want to be clear about that. But she kept missing her word.

Here's the part that sat wrong with me. She set the targets. She picked the deadlines. Not me. And then she'd come up short.

When I set mine, I hit them. Even when life was on fire.

She had a line for it. Shooting for the stars. Even if you under-deliver, the progress is still good.

It sounds generous. It's also a way to never be wrong.

I get it. Life happens. A demanding new job, a hundred things pulling at you at once. I've been there. I'm there now, same as her.

But I still find the thirty minutes.

Because that's what the thirty minutes measures. Not your calendar. Your priorities.

The worst part? I wasn't walking in to end it. The opposite. I had a plan to hand her a whole side of the business — her as CEO of one half, me feeding her the first clients to get it off the ground. A door built for her to walk through.

But if a thirty-minute call is impossible, the door doesn't matter.

Two years taught me one thing. The other person commits fully to the path, or the exit is swift. There's no long middle where one of you carries the weight and calls it a partnership.

And the exit conditions? You agree them on day one. When everyone's excited and nobody thinks they'll need them. Because by the time you do need them, you're too tangled to think straight.

Three strikes and you're out.

I just wish I'd written the strikes down two years ago.

Keep building. -Ochoa
