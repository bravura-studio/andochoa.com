# Deep Dive: The Freedom Gap

Revisited the September 2025 voice note about the BUILD.FUN.FREE mantra and the freedom gap — the feeling of having nailed BUILD and FUN but not FREEDOM.

## Q&A

**Q: That voice note was from September. You were "fighting for the next day." Seven months later — is the freedom gap bigger, smaller, or just different?**

A: [Founder chose to write this as a historical piece, capturing September 2025 as it was.]

**Q: You said "going back is a thought that haunts me." Haunts you how — fear of having to go back, or fear that you might WANT to go back?**

A: The fear is still very real. I might have to go back because I was not successful in achieving a way to sustain myself through my endeavours. Every time I think about going back I feel trapped, sad and lose energy. It is like going into a depressive state. That I've lost my way and I am sailing to nowhere, completely lost in the sea and nowhere to go to.

**Q: In that flow state — building late at night, skipping meals — did the fear disappear? Or was it always there?**

A: It disappears while I am at it. The feeling comes back when I snap out of it and start overthinking. What am I doing? Am I focusing on the right thing? Am I doing the right stuff? What if I put all this effort and this leads me to nothing? It is this feeling of maybe not doing/taking the right steps that creates stress. Because time goes by and you never know if your efforts will ever amount to anything that gives you the signal in the middle of all noise that will allow you to break free at last.

**Q: Give me a specific moment where you thought "this person likes this but they don't NEED this."**

A: All the conversations ended up with "this is interesting," but as soon we presented price the mood suddenly changed and they need to think about it. Then took a lot of time to make a decision and even wanted to negotiate the price down or asked for special conditions even though we were already offering special conditions for being an early adopter. Seems that they need to see it whole to believe it and are not a fit for being a design partner and build alongside you. Then after some time you hear that they went with another solution that does not what they wanted but it is a branded name at an expensive price, while we were offering to build alongside them what they needed at a fraction of the cost. Maybe we were just talking to the wrong crowd? Maybe we were not passing our message clear enough? Because I read all this "sell before build" all over the internet. I tried that several times and with different approaches, but never was very successful with it. Does this only work in the USA market? Maybe the pain is not strong enough? Maybe they don't trust in us?
