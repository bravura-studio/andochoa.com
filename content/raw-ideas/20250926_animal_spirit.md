# The Animal Spirit — Deep Dive

Revisited the September 2025 voice note about the "animal spirit" — the entrepreneurial drive that kept creating friction in corporate and startup environments, until the decision to stop suppressing it.

## Q&A

**Q: That colleague who said "I cut all candidates who want to launch their own business" — you said it came from someone you trusted. What was the relationship like BEFORE that moment? And did it change anything between you two, or did you just swallow it?**

A: He was the head of a department that I worked closely with during my time at Sonae. We both had a great passion for football. He was a great guy. Also, he helped me get my first football coach position at his hometown club for one of their youth teams. We always had a great relationship and still have. Not much in touch with him now, but no friction between us. When he said that I just swallowed it. Then I was cautious around him about mentioning anything about my entrepreneurial endeavours and hopes.

**Q: You mentioned trying intrapreneurship — thinking with an owner's perspective inside other people's companies. Was there ever a moment where it actually WORKED? Where you pushed, someone listened, and the thing got better because of you?**

A: Sometimes it worked and when I had the necessary degrees of freedom to implement what I wanted it yielded good results — like a time at Knok where we divided into squads and inside the squad I pushed for my ideas, they got implemented and it thrived. We were the best squad in terms of business outcomes and team happiness. Even team members from the other squad wanted to join ours. Although, mostly at Sonae — a big corp — it was always friction. More often than not. I would go to my manager with new projects and ideas and his reply was always the same: thanks, that's great, I will take it to upper management and let's see. Then complete radio silence and the roadmap was completely different from what we had planned with him.

**Q: You said at the startups it hurt MORE than the corporate layoffs. Do you think the founders were threatened by you? Or was it something else?**

A: I believe that they wanted yes-men. Because the founders almost had a god complex. That they were the ones always right. They wanted people to nod along and execute their vision. Usually I am ok with that — the founders should own the vision. But when that vision is not working, maybe you should listen to your team and try a different approach. They always came to the team with certainties that never materialised afterwards. But then pushed us into that direction anyway. At least until the funding dried out and they had to perform layoffs.

**Q: You called it "the animal spirit" — not ambition, not hustle, not drive. Is it something you can't turn off? Or was there a point where you consciously decided "I'm done suppressing this"?**

A: Both. This is what it feels like — the primal instinct. But I reached a point where I consciously decided that I was done and embraced it.
