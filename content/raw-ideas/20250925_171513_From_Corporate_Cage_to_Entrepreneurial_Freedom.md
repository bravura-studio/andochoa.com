# From Corporate Cage to Entrepreneurial Freedom

## Original Transcription

My previous life, I had a couple of jobs ranging from consultancy, investment banking, controller, and then product manager at tech companies. I always thought that this was the way to go, go get a job, go to college, go get a job, improve continuously, learn new skills and progress on the corporate ladder. And this was the best set from my parents, I think, from a young age. But here and there, I always thought that maybe there was another way because I was on a comfortable cage. I always tried to learn continuously and I went for the jobs that I think that could where I could learn the most. More specifically, coding, even if I'm not from a technical background, since my background is economics and finance. But I was always thinking and always trying to launch side projects because. When I looked at things, I think it could be improved, saw opportunities, business opportunities, and thinking always in my head, maybe I can do this and that and that. Experimented a lot, failed a lot through all these side projects. But then came a moment of realization that I was moving from job to job. Sometimes I was laid off like three times and all these three times were just corporates trying to reduce costs because of world events, externalities that affected me directly but where even if I was a good professional, I was getting promoted every year, I was getting raised every year. But at the end, it didn't matter and I got laid off anyway. So it was unbearable to continue like this. And I said, OK, maybe now it's time to launch myself, my own thing. And I saw the future of this AI where now I can have an idea and call that idea with the help of AI to build something, to build a product that I am able to sell. And in the future, I don't want to be stuck in this office, on this corporate world, going through this corporate ladder meaningless. I want to build my own thing. I want to have fun doing it, to choose the people I can work with, the times I can work with whom and where and when. So I decided to quit the corporate world and start this entrepreneurial journey. So this is where the transformation begins. And my current reality is, well, I'm still figuring these things out. I don't know where I'm going. I know that I'm experimenting and getting feedback and improving and learning new skills until I find something that resonates and gets traction. Now I'm sacrificing a little bit in terms of relationships, money, comfort, a relationship with my wife that is a little bit scared of the path that I've chosen. But I know that I need to be resilient because I'm building these new skills. I'm building new relationships. And I think that this takes time and I just need time and opportunities, but I'm going to grab them.

## The Person Behind the Pixels 👤

Most people see the polished LinkedIn profiles, the career progression, the steady promotions. What they don't see is the growing sense of suffocation that comes with trading your time for someone else's vision, over and over again.

I'm someone who followed the "right" path for years—and discovered it was completely wrong for me.

---

### BEFORE: The Golden Handcuffs Were Real

**My Previous Life**: Investment banking → consultancy → controller → product manager at tech companies. The classic corporate ladder climb that my parents (rightfully) believed was the path to security.

**The Comfortable Cage**: Getting promoted every year. Getting raises every year. Learning continuously. Picking jobs where I could grow the most—even teaching myself to code despite my economics and finance background.

**Internal Struggles**: That nagging voice asking "is this it?" Every time I saw a business opportunity, I'd think "maybe I can do this better." Side projects filled my evenings and weekends. Failed experiments littered my Google Drive.

**Raw Truth**: *I was successful by every external measure but felt like I was slowly dying inside. The security felt more like quicksand.*

---

### THE CALL: When "Safe" Became Dangerous

**The Moment**: Getting laid off. Not once. Not twice. **Three times.**

**The Pain**: Here's what broke me—it didn't matter that I was good at my job. Didn't matter that I got promoted yearly, raised yearly, praised consistently. When corporate needed to cut costs due to "world events" and "externalities," I was just a number on a spreadsheet.

**The Vision**: I saw AI changing everything. For the first time in history, someone with an idea could actually build and sell a product without a massive team. I could see a future where I chose who I worked with, when I worked, where I worked.

**The Decision**: *"It was unbearable to continue like this. Maybe now it's time to launch my own thing."*

---

### AFTER: The Messy Reality of Freedom

**Current Reality**: I'm figuring it out as I go. I don't have a clear roadmap. I'm experimenting, getting feedback, learning new skills, hoping something will resonate and get traction.

**What I've Sacrificed**: 
- **Money**: No more steady paycheck
- **Comfort**: No more predictable days
- **Relationships**: My wife is scared of this path I've chosen
- **Security**: Everything feels uncertain now

**What I've Gained**: 
- **Skills**: Building things I never thought I could
- **Relationships**: Connecting with other builders and entrepreneurs  
- **Perspective**: Understanding what I actually want from life
- **Hope**: For the first time in years, I'm excited about the future

**Today's Truth**: *I still don't know where I'm going, but I'm finally moving in the right direction. Building new skills takes time. New relationships take time. I just need to be resilient and grab opportunities when they come.*

---

### THE BRIDGE: Why I'm Sharing This Journey

**Why Share**: Because someone out there is in their own comfortable cage, wondering if there's another way. I want them to know they're not alone in feeling trapped by "success."

**What To Expect**: The unfiltered reality of transitioning from employee to entrepreneur. The wins, the losses, the 2 AM anxiety, the moments of clarity, the relationship challenges, the money stress—all of it.

**My Promise**: I'll share the real numbers, the real struggles, the real lessons. No humble bragging, no fake vulnerability. Just blood, sweat, and tears—because that's what building something actually looks like.

---

## What This Means for You

If you're reading this from your office, wondering if there's more to life than climbing someone else's ladder, here's what I wish someone had told me:

**The "safe" path isn't safe anymore.** I learned this the hard way through three layoffs. Your job security is an illusion.

**The tools exist now.** AI, no-code platforms, online marketplaces—everything you need to build something is available. The barriers have never been lower.

**It's scary as hell, and that's normal.** My wife is scared. I'm scared. But I'd rather be scared and building something meaningful than comfortable and slowly dying inside.

**You don't need to have it all figured out.** I certainly don't. But you need to start somewhere.
