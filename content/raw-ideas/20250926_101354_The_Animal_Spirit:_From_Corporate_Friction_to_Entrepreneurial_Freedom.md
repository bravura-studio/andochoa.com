# The Animal Spirit: When "Entrepreneurial Mindset" Gets You Fired

*A raw story about corporate culture, startup disillusionment, and the moment I decided enough was enough.*

---

## The Betrayal

*"I cut all job candidates that say that in the future they want to launch their own business. They have no place here."*

Those words hit differently when they come from a close colleague. Someone I trusted. Someone I thought shared my values.

But in that moment, I realized we lived in completely different worlds.

## The Pattern

I've always had what I call the "animal spirit" - this relentless drive to build, to solve problems, to take ownership. It's why I launched side projects, participated in hackathons, and constantly suggested improvements at work.

I genuinely cared about the businesses I worked for. I spoke my mind not to cause trouble, but because I wanted to make things better.

The result? Multiple layoffs.

The feedback was always the same: *"Difficult to work with." "Not a team player." "Too many side projects."*

What they really meant was: *"Not a yes-man."*

## The Startup Myth

After hearing that colleague's comment about cutting entrepreneurial candidates, I thought I had it figured out. I needed to find my people. I needed startups - places where entrepreneurial thinking would be valued.

I was wrong.

**Startup #1:** Founders with a god complex who couldn't handle different viewpoints. Even their "entrepreneurial" culture wanted conformity.

**Startup #2:** Hired me to lead product development. Gave me direct reports and degrees of freedom to make changes. But when I pushed for necessary changes while the status quo was failing, they weren't aligned. When funding became tight at the end of the ZIRP cycle, guess who got cut?

The most painful part? I was pushing hardest when the road got tough. I had the position, the team, and the drive to make real changes. But I needed founder backing, and it wasn't there.

## Enough Was Enough

Those final two startup layoffs hurt the most. Not because of the job loss, but because of what they represented.

Even in supposedly "entrepreneurial" environments, the animal spirit was still unwelcome. The pattern was clear: whether corporate or startup, systems want executors, not owners.

That's when it hit me: **I need to build my own thing.**

Not execute someone else's vision. Not try to be an "owner" within someone else's company. Actually own something.

## Setting the Animal Spirit Free

The colleagues who worked closely with me always said I was a good professional. They valued the owner's mentality I brought. But the decision-makers - the ones viewing from the outside without context - they saw friction.

Now I'm done trying to fit my animal spirit into boxes designed for yes-men.

I'm building in public. Documenting the real journey. Blood, sweat, and tears - no fluff.

Because maybe, just maybe, I'm not the only one with this "problem."

## To My Fellow Entrepreneurs

If you're reading this and nodding along, I have a question for you:

**Is this normal? Have you faced this same rejection of true entrepreneurial thinking - not just in corporations, but in startups too?**

**How many layoffs did it take before you decided to build your own thing?**

I'm documenting this journey not just for myself, but for everyone who's been told their animal spirit is a problem rather than an asset.

Let's build something together. Let's prove that owner's mentality isn't a bug - it's a feature.
