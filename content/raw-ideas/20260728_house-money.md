# Deep Dive: House Money

## Q1 — What's driving you right now?

Keep building, keep shipping. Whenever I can I command the agents to one more task. Last weekend I shipped like crazy. I'm finalising the last touches on striva-dental to make it production ready and deploy it on the first client — already scheduled for Friday.

*(GitHub check: striva-dental — 237 commits in 10 days. 51 Saturday, 81 Sunday = 132 in the weekend. Almost all agent-driven via the `defract` task pipeline. Work is CI hardening, alembic round-trips, deploy-approved-SHA, prod env vars — hardening for a real client, not new features.)*

## Q2 — Two years, no revenue, a PM job for the bills, and Friday the first real client touches striva-dental. What actually shows up when you think about Friday — relief, dread, or the itch to command one more task so you don't have to sit in it?

It's that feeling of being alive all over again. Ship, ship and ship. No matter what, to make sure it's ready and put it in front of real users. Other products have been in the hands of real users, and striva-app is in production with real users — but we have shy of 10 and that barely pays the bills.

Now striva-dental is a renewed hope. This might pan out. We have the right partnership to make this work. Start with one client and keep adding more and more. But now my timeline is calmer. Don't need to add one more every day. It can slowly compound to something great and all of a sudden explode — and that's the moment I've been daydreaming about.

## Q3 — You've already done the 900-commit year on striva-app and it landed at 10 users. Same fire, same daydream of the explosion. So which is actually keeping you alive — the shipping, or the belief that this time it explodes? If dental also lands at 10 users a year from now, does the aliveness survive?

*(GitHub check: striva-app — ~900 commits over the last year. 196 in May, 136 in March, 129 in December. A wall of shipping → fewer than 10 users. July went quiet, 6 commits, because the firehose moved to dental.)*

Let me add more colour here. The timeline is calmer regarding the pressure to add more users.

I still feel the fire of building. Anything. I like to build. If what I build gets me the freedom I long for — the better. But now the PMing pays the bills and I don't have to worry about that. So everything that comes my way from my building is a great bonus. Let it compound.

Because it is always the same pattern I see everywhere I look — except for some rare occasions that products catch fire, but those are the exception and not the norm. Even the most prolific builders from the indie hackers community took multiple attempts and years until they found the one that grew like a rocket.

## Core insight

The contradiction (frantic 132-commit weekend vs. "calmer timeline") resolves: the fire is about *building*, not *winning*. The PM job didn't kill the dream — it freed it. When survival stopped riding on the build, the build got lighter. He can ship hard AND wait years for the explosion, because the aliveness lives in the building, not the outcome. Playing with house money.
