There are two kinds of pain.

The first one drains you. You sit at a desk doing work that doesn't matter, for people who don't care, inside a machine that would replace you tomorrow. You go home empty. I lived that for years. Got promoted every year. Got a raise every year. Got laid off three times anyway.

The second kind fills you up.

I skip lunch. I work through the night. Everything breaks at the same time — the product, the partnership, the plan. I look at my bank account and for the first time in my life I have to dip into savings.

And yet. I have never felt so alive.

The honeymoon ended a long time ago. I'm 1.5 years into building my own thing and I've thought about quitting more times than I can count. Just get a regular job. Cruise through life.

But the thought of going back haunts me more than the struggle of going forward.

Because this pain — the builder's pain — is fuel. Every day I'm getting better and faster. I can feel it compounding in ways I can't fully see yet.

If my kids read this someday: create your own belief system. When everything is falling apart, look up to your north star. Today is not permanent. It's a stepping stone.
