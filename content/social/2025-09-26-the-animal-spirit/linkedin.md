A colleague I respected once told me he cuts every job candidate who mentions wanting to start a business someday. "They have no place here."

He wasn't being cruel. He was being honest. And that honesty taught me more than any performance review.

I swallowed it. Stopped mentioning side projects around him. We still got along. Still talked football. But I'd learned to hide a part of myself at work.

I call it the animal spirit. Not ambition. Not hustle. Something more primal. This compulsion to see problems and build solutions. To take ownership even when nobody asked.

I tried channeling it inside companies. At the big corp, every idea got "I'll take it to upper management" and then radio silence. At a startup where I had real freedom, it worked — best squad, best outcomes, people wanting to join.

The animal spirit wasn't the problem. The cage was.

Corporate or startup — most systems want executors, not owners. I stopped looking for permission to use it. Now I build my own thing.

The friction didn't disappear. It just became mine.

Have you ever had to hide the part of yourself that wants to build?
