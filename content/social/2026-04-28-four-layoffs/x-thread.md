I got laid off four times in five years.

For a long time the question was: why me?

It took all four to realize I was asking the wrong question.

---

Layoff 1: An AI recruitment company in London. Picked me out of 400 candidates. Day one was onboarding. Day two I got a call — company being acquired, keeping only people needed for the tech transition.

I'd been there one day.

---

Layoff 2: A trading company that wanted a product manager. Two days in they had me running ops collecting waste oil from restaurants. Every morning the thought of going in made me physically sick. Actual vomiting.

My body rejected the place before my mind caught up.

---

Layoffs 3 and 4: Two startups, both Series A, both during the tail end of the ZIRP era.

Both times I was delivering. Both times VCs tightened, headcount got cut.

Same macro forces. Same outcome. Different logo.

---

The pattern: acquisition, cultural mismatch, macro economics, macro economics again.

Not one was about my performance.

The new question: why am I giving 100% to build someone else's thing when the outcome is controlled by forces I can't influence?

The only real safety is building something where you control the variables.
