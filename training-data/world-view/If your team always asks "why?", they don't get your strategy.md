If your team always asks "why?", they don't get your strategy.

So what can you do?

(Hint: it's not showing them the roadmap.)

🧵👇

![The image is a flowchart titled ](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GuX4XNRaQAAFkm2?format=jpg&name=large)

---

**Carl Vellotti** @carlvellotti [2025-06-26](https://x.com/carlvellotti/status/1938228308573041126)

Let's agree:

1️⃣ Product development depends on 𝗮𝗹𝗶𝗴𝗻𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁

And:

2️⃣ 𝗔𝗹𝗶𝗴𝗻𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 requires 𝘂𝗻𝗱𝗲𝗿𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗻𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴

So persistent "why are we building this?" questions reveal a lack of understanding.

---

**Carl Vellotti** @carlvellotti [2025-06-26](https://x.com/carlvellotti/status/1938228320472363396)

Telling them it's critical is patronizing and won't work. You need alignment BEFORE that meeting.

What should you discuss instead?

To get buy-in, explain the reasoning behind each decision.

**Carl Vellotti** @carlvellotti [2025-06-26](https://x.com/carlvellotti/status/1938228332535112016)

Imagine this:

𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗽𝗼𝘀𝗲 𝗮 𝗻𝗲𝘄 𝗮𝗻𝗮𝗹𝘆𝘁𝗶𝗰𝘀 𝗱𝗮𝘀𝗵𝗯𝗼𝗮𝗿𝗱.

Engineers ask:

𝘞𝘩𝘺 𝘥𝘰 𝘸𝘦 𝘯𝘦𝘦𝘥 𝘵𝘩𝘪𝘴?

𝘞𝘰𝘯'𝘵 𝘪𝘵 𝘥𝘶𝘱𝘭𝘪𝘤𝘢𝘵𝘦 𝘦𝘹𝘪𝘴𝘵𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘧𝘦𝘢𝘵𝘶𝘳𝘦𝘴?

❌ Saying "it's key for Q4 goals!" won't convince them.

---

**Carl Vellotti** @carlvellotti [2025-06-26](https://x.com/carlvellotti/status/1938228344321151481)

What could work is explaining:

1️⃣ Users need more conversion funnel visibility

2️⃣ Current tools don't provide that

3️⃣ This dashboard unlocks those insights

---

**Carl Vellotti** @carlvellotti [2025-06-26](https://x.com/carlvellotti/status/1938228356274909319)

That context gets your team aligned.

The roadmap matters, but nailing the "Why" comes first.

Don't patronize your team by stating the obvious.
