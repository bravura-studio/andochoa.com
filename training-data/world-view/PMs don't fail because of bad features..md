PMs don't fail because of bad features.

They fail because they can't explain why a decision was made.

Here's a framework I use to bulletproof decisions 👇

---

**George from prodmgmt.world** @nurijanian [2025-08-23](https://x.com/nurijanian/status/1959299872869466418)

Before any big decision, ask these 3 questions:

1\. "If we shipped this and it failed completely, what would we learn?"

2\. "What's the smallest version that proves the core assumption?"

3\. "Who specifically asked for this, and how often?"

---

**George from prodmgmt.world** @nurijanian [2025-08-23](https://x.com/nurijanian/status/1959299884684824619)

Question #1 turns failures into data.

Question #2 prevents over-engineering.

Question #3 separates real demand from noise.

Most "failed" features actually succeed at teaching you something valuable.

---

**George from prodmgmt.world** @nurijanian [2025-08-23](https://x.com/nurijanian/status/1959299896777015468)

This shifts you from "feature factory" mode → strategic PM.

Instead of shipping what's asked for, you're shipping what teaches you the most about your users' real problems.
